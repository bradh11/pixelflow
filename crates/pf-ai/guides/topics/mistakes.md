# Common mistakes

The usual ways a sequence goes wrong, how to spot each in your own draft, and what to do instead.

## Everything on, all the time

Every prop lit at full from the first bar to the last. It looks impressive for eight seconds and then like nothing, because there is no way for it to get bigger.

- Spot it: every section in your plan lights every prop; `review_draft` says every prop is lit most of the song.
- Instead: give each section a level (topic `intensity`), and rest most of the house in the quiet ones (topic `layering`).

## Effect soup

A different effect on every prop, changed every few bars, none related to the others. Each is fine; together they are noise.

- Spot it: you can't say in one sentence what a section looks like.
- Instead: two or three kinds of effect a section, several props sharing each. The same look for the same music.

## A rainbow in every section

Six colors at once because six were available.

- Spot it: `review_draft` counts a section's colors; more than four is flagged.
- Instead: two or three colors, held for the section and changed at the next (topic `palettes`).

## Strobing

Fast flashes used as the default way to show excitement. It tires the eye, it reads as a fault, and across a large area it is a safety problem.

- Spot it: `strobe`, `shimmer`, or very short on-off effects cut on every beat or half beat for bars on end.
- Instead: movement and color for energy; flashes for single moments. Topic `safety` has the limits.

## Nothing changes

The same look from the first verse through the last chorus, perhaps a little faster.

- Spot it: `review_draft` says a section looks the same as the one before it, or that props hold one effect for many bars.
- Instead: something visibly changes on the first beat of every section (topic `intensity`).

## Everything changes, constantly

The opposite fault: a new effect on every prop every bar. Nothing lasts long enough to be seen.

- Spot it: hundreds of effects one bar long on every row.
- Instead: let the base hold for the section. Cut only one layer, or one prop group, on bars or beats.

## Ignoring half the props

The tree and the matrix do everything; the windows, the canes, and the star are never lit, or are lit once.

- Spot it: `review_draft` names props that never light.
- Instead: give every prop a part, at least in the loud sections (topic `roles`).

## The wrong effect for the prop

Fire on a roofline. Text on an arch. A picture on a string of fifty pixels. A singing face on a prop with no face.

- Spot it: the effect's guide says what it draws on each kind of prop; `review_draft` names effects on props they don't suit.
- Instead: effects that draw pictures go on matrices and trees. Single runs of pixels get the effects that travel along them.

## Text nobody can read

A sentence scrolled across a small matrix in two seconds, or six-letter words on a matrix four letters wide.

- Spot it: `review_draft` checks letter height, width, speed, and time on screen.
- Instead: short words, held still when they fit, scrolled slowly when they don't (the `text` guide has the numbers).

## Missing the moments

A beautiful base look that sails straight through the drop, the stop, and the big hit as if they weren't there.

- Spot it: `review_draft` lists the top moments that don't show.
- Instead: `stage_cue`, and make the two or three biggest moments bigger than everything else (topic `cues`).

## Hits with nothing to land from

A flash at full brightness out of a look that was already at full brightness.

- Spot it: `review_draft` calls it flat.
- Instead: dim or go dark for the half beat before.

## Lights during silence

Props lit before the music starts, through a full stop, or after the last note.

- Instead: dark when the music is silent (topic `openings-endings`).

## Off the grid

Effects that start a little before or after the beat because the times were guessed.

- Instead: cut on timing tracks (`place_effects` with `track`) rather than typing times. PixelFlow moves new edges that are within a beat of a mark onto it before the user sees them, so aim close and it will finish the job.

## Too clever

Five layers, three blends, and curves on four settings to make something that reads, from the street, as "the tree is blue". If a simpler look reads the same at a distance, use the simpler look. The user can edit two effects; they can't untangle twenty.
