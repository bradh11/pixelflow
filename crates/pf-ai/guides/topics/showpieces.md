# Showpieces and range

A concept from the song, the effects people remember and where each belongs, and the whole range of effects used across a show without putting any of them where it doesn't suit.

## Start from the song

Before choosing a single effect, find the show's idea in the song itself: its title, its words, its mood. "Paper lanterns in the snow" suggests warm lights rising through falling snow; a song about a storm, lightning and rain; a lullaby, slow clouds of color and starlight. Say it to yourself in a few words, and let it choose the showpieces and the colors.

Consider more than the first palette that comes to mind. Red and green, or orange and purple, is one choice of several (topic `palettes`): a winter song can be ice blue and silver, a warm one gold and amber, a spooky one green and violet. Whatever you choose, hold each section to two or three colors.

## Signature moments

Every song gets at least one showpiece on each canvas (a matrix, a mega tree): an effect that draws a picture of something rather than a color, placed where the music earns it. Starting points, each a ready-made look from that effect's guide (read the guide before placing it):

| Where in the song | Effect and look | On |
|---|---|---|
| A breakdown or a bridge | `plasma` "Icy clouds" | matrix, tree |
| A breakdown or a bridge | `shader` "Northern lights on the matrix" | matrix, tree |
| The biggest hit or the drop | `fireworks` "One huge burst from the middle of the house" | matrix |
| The biggest hit or the drop | `shockwave` "White-hot ring cooling to blue" | matrix, tree |
| A chorus | `dancer` "Santa bouncing on the beat" | matrix |
| A chorus | `picture` "A picture held on a matrix" | matrix |
| A chorus | `butterfly` "Rainbow wings" | matrix, tree |
| A quiet verse | `snowflakes` "Gentle snowfall" | matrix, tree |
| A quiet verse | `meteors` "Icy rain" | matrix, tree |
| A warm verse or a fireside bridge | `fire` "A low hearth fire" | matrix, tree |
| A build | `meteors` "Sparks rising" | tree, matrix |
| The last chorus | `fireworks` "A firework finale on a matrix" | matrix, tree |
| An intro or an ending | `garlands` "A tree filling from the foot" | tree, matrix |

- A showpiece holds the canvas for a while: a section, a few bars, or the beat of a hit (a burst or a shockwave is an effect a second or two long). Put it on the canvas's own row, on a layer above the section's look or in its place.
- Give it the section's colors where the effect takes colors, and the section's brightness (topic `intensity`): a quiet verse's snowfall is dim, the finale's fireworks full.
- The user's own pictures and GIFs are the best showpiece of all: `picture` with one of the context block's pictures.
- One showpiece for the whole song stops being a moment. Choose a different one for each kind of section that has one.
- Words and a singing face are not showpieces: they come as well, where the song has words (topic `vocals`).

## Range across a show

A show that plays the same handful of effects in every section looks the same from start to finish, however well it keeps time. Use the range.

- Each different part of the song (verse, chorus, bridge) gets its own effects on some props, not only its own colors. A section never plays exactly the same effects as the section before it.
- Repeats of the same music keep their look, with a variation: the second chorus looks like the first. Range comes from the different parts, and from the last chorus adding its showpiece.
- About ten kinds of effect suit a song of a minute and a half to three minutes on a display with a matrix and a tree: fewer on a small display, a few more on a long song. They come from the sections' looks, the movement over them, the showpieces, the singing, and the cues.
- Range never means an effect where it doesn't suit: each kind of prop has its own range, below. `review_draft` scores the range, names a canvas with no showpiece, and gives the call that places one.

## What suits what

Outlines, rooflines, and arches are one row of pixels: give them motion that travels along the line. Besides `chase`, `wave`, and `twinkle`:

| Effect and look | On |
|---|---|
| `bars` "Candy stripe along an outline" | string, arch |
| `wipe` "New color sweeping in" | string, arch |
| `pinwheel` "Bands running out along an outline" | string, arch |
| `garlands` "An outline filling end to end" | string, arch |
| `meteors` "A comet along an outline" | string, arch |
| `morph` "Opening from the middle of an outline" | string, arch |
| `fill` "Level bar along a roofline" | string, arch |
| `circles` "Runners on an outline" | string, arch |
| `fire` "Burning along an outline" | string, arch |
| `shockwave` "Blast on a hit" | string, arch |

These have no look for a single row of pixels. On an outline or an arch they show a slice of themselves at best, and the review flags them there: keep them on a matrix, or the whole house where their guide says so.
