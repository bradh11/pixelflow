# Concepts

Before a whole show or a new look: two or three different ideas for it, sketched in a few lines, and one chosen on purpose rather than the first that came to mind.

## When to sketch them

- A whole show for a song, or a new look for one ("something different", "a new look for the choruses"): sketch them before choosing any effect.
- The person already named the idea or the colors ("make it spooky purple", "candy cane red and white"): that is the concept. Sketch two or three takes on it (spooky purple as a haunted mist, as a storm, as a graveyard glow), or none when it is already clear enough to build.
- The person asks for one of your earlier concepts ("try the second one", "the river idea"): build that one as you sketched it, with no new sketches. With a proposal still open, start again with `reset_draft`.
- A question, a recolor, a small edit, a fix, or a job about levels: no sketches. Get on with it.

## What a concept is

A line or two each:

- **The idea**: one image or story taken from the song: its title, a line of its words, its mood, its tempo, the way it is built (a quiet start and a big finish, a stop, a last chorus that lifts).
- **The palette**: two or three colors, and what each is for. It comes from the idea; the palettes in topic `palettes` are starting points, not the only ones.
- **The motion**: how it feels: drifting, marching, falling, bursting, flickering, breathing.
- **Effects that could say it**: a few from the whole range (topic `range`), picked for this idea. They are your choice, not a requirement, and change freely once you build.

## Make them different

Different ideas, not one idea in other colors: each has its own image, its own palette, its own kind of motion, and mostly its own effects. The obvious reading of the song can be one of them; the others come from somewhere else in it. If two share a palette or most of their effects, replace one.

Places to find another reading:

- the title taken literally, then as a feeling;
- one image from the words, or from a single line;
- the structure: what the stop, the build, and the last chorus could be in a story;
- the tempo and the drums: something that marches, something that floats;
- the display: what the matrix, the tree, or the arches could be in this story (a window, a sky, a stage, a clock).

## Choosing one

Choose the one that best suits the song, what the person asked for, and this display: a concept built around a matrix needs a matrix; one built on a single color across the house needs enough props to carry it. The obvious one wins only when it is the best, not for being safe. Any of them, done well, makes a good show; `review_draft` judges how it is built, not which concept it is.

## Saying it

Write them for the person in a few short lines, numbered, each with a short name:

1. Name: the idea. Colors: ... Motion: ... Effects: ...
2. ...

then one sentence: which you chose, and why. Write it in the same reply as your next tool call (reading the guides of the effects you chose), and carry on: don't stop to ask which they want. They see the finished show, and can ask for one of the others.

Then the concept you chose leads the plan (topic `method`, step 4): its palette for each section, its motion, its effects where they suit the props. The summary you give `propose_changes` opens with the concept you built and why, in a sentence, so the person knows there were others to ask for.
